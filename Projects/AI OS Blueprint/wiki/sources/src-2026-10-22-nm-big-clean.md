# Video 1: "How to Organize Your Files Better Than 99% of People"

**What it is:** a ~14-minute YouTube video by [[nick-milo]] on his yearly "Big Clean": tidy the physical desk, the computer's files, then the Obsidian vault. Record: `raw/videos/2026-10-22-nm-big-clean.md`.

## Key takeaways
1. Outer clutter (desk, desktop, a downloads folder with thousands of files, an overflowing vault) drains the ability to think clearly; clean it in three passes: physical → digital → thinking space ([[aios-big-clean]]).
2. Digital files: dump the desktop into one folder in seconds, then keep a single **"+" (plus) folder** in your synced storage holding one *single source* for downloads and one for screenshots ([[aios-plus-folder]]).
3. Don't hand-sort downloads: sort by size, review only files over **10 MB** for deletion, then at year end sweep everything into a dated year folder (e.g. "2026 downloads (n)") with the item count in the name.
4. Files matter less than notes. In Obsidian he keeps a "+" folder for unsorted new notes and processes it in bulk: search by path, copy the results as wiki links into a note, batch-move obvious groups (e.g. 25 notes about one subject, all people notes), then open only the notes worth thinking about.
5. Organise notes **for context**, not tidiness: a note is findable when it is well linked (via `[[` autocomplete), belongs to a collection, and sits in a sensible folder such as Atlas ([[aios-ace-framework]], [[aios-maps-of-content]]).

## Details worth keeping
- Name the browsers' download folder slightly differently ("Downloadss") so you always know which one is the single source.
- Screenshot tool used: CleanShot X (Mac). Year folders let you compare volume year to year.
- The Big Clean is also run as a live yearly workshop in his paid Knowledge Academy (promotional segment).
